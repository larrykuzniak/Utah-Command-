Field manual

Your field manual

Scenarios: Use Scenarios to switch battles. Each has its own map, forces, supply allowance, air missions, and deadline. One battle per scenario saves on this device. Resume through Scenarios; New game restarts only the current scenario. Export for an independent backup. All maps and force mixes are illustrative.

Mission: Advance scenarios end immediately if you secure all three flags, or give a limited victory for holding two at the deadline. Hold the Beachhead requires at least two flags at the end of turn 10; holding all three does not end that defensive scenario early. Flags retain ownership until occupied by the other side.

Situation reports: Opening briefings and reports after every turn are available from Situation report. Choose an older report to review it. Automatic reports can be turned off in that window. Reports include friendly strength changes, supplies, air missions, reinforcements, objectives, and observed enemy activity. Enemy intelligence respects fog of war.

Illustrated map: The continuous Normandy illustration follows the first concept map. Switch on Terrain overlay to see the exact movement terrain of each game hex. Roads and buildings in the background artwork are illustrative; game terrain and movement costs are defined by the overlay. The same battles and saved positions are used.

Movement preview: Gold-bordered hexes show where the selected unit can move this turn after projected supply delivery. Hidden enemy units may still block an advance.

  1. Tap an American counter (or its name in the unit list).
  2. Choose Move, then tap a destination. The dashed line shows your planned route. Units advance as far as their movement allowance permits; issue another order next turn to continue.
  3. Or choose Attack, then tap an enemy. Infantry and tanks attack adjacent hexes; artillery reaches four hexes.
  4. Issue orders to your other units, then tap Resolve turn. The Germans respond automatically.

Fog of war & intelligence: Toggle fog above the map at any time. Infantry and tanks spot enemy units within 3 hexes; artillery spots within 2. Spotting is shared by each side. With Operational depth off, terrain does not block line of sight; turn it on through Operations for hedgerow and town obstruction. Dimmed terrain is outside current observation. A dashed question-mark counter is a last-known position, not a confirmed unit. It disappears when troops search that hex and find it empty. Intelligence lists current sightings and the turn and hex of older contacts. Air and ground attacks require a spotted target with fog on; lost air targets preserve the mission. Turning fog off reveals all current enemies, but intelligence records only actual troop observations. The German computer follows the same spotting ranges when fog is on. All remaining units are shown when the battle ends.

Counter colors: Americans are blue; Germans are gray. Red is reserved for British and Canadian forces. Canadian units are available in the Juno scenario. Counter symbols and INF / ARM / ART labels distinguish infantry, armor, and artillery. The selected unit is enlarged, marked by a bright gold halo and SELECTED nameplate, and drawn over a dimmed map. The unit list and supply list also highlight your selection. Select a unit by its name in either list; Clear selection above the map removes the highlight.

Movement: Infantry 3, tanks 4, artillery 2 points. Marsh and hedgerows cost 2; other land costs 1. Units cannot stack or pass through occupied hexes.

German opponent: The computer guards objectives, coordinates target selection to reduce wasted fire, moves reserves toward threatened flags, positions artillery for ranged fire, and withdraws damaged units when a safer hex is available. Safe units may rest to recover. With fog on, tactical decisions use current German sightings; the computer cannot read your queued orders.

Scenario builder: Open Scenario builder in the header. Copy the current battlefield or start with a blank field map. Set the name, turns, supplies, air support, allied faction, and victory condition. Select a tool to paint terrain, place or remove units, or move each of the three flags and set its initial owner. HQs are added automatically when playing. Save stores the design on this browser and adds it to Scenarios. Save & play starts a new battle and asks before replacing a saved battle for the same design. Export and Import transfer a scenario design independently of game saves. A design already used in a battle is saved under a new identifier when edited, preserving the old battle. The workshop keeps up to twelve saved designs.

Operational depth: Open Operations in the header and enable Operational depth. This also enables Advanced Rules. With it off, classic movement and observation remain available. Roads connecting road/town hexes cost half a movement point; marsh and hedgerows cost two. Crowded beaches (at least five formations on beach terrain) cost two to enter.

Line of sight: Intermediate hedgerow and town hexes block observation; adjacent hexes remain visible. Spotting is shared. Indirect artillery fire requires a friendly observer even with fog disabled. Smoke still reduces losses without blocking observation.

Rivers and bridges: The blue river boundary and labeled crossings are exact operational overlays. Infantry can ford for three movement points (two with engineers). Engineers also let tanks ford for two. Artillery, HQs, and supply transport require intact bridges. Damaged crossing edges are impassable; infantry may ford elsewhere. Adjacent engineer-capable troops can Repair or Demolish through Operations. Engineering resolves before movement and consumes two supply points.

Zones and reserves: Infantry and armor exert an adjacent zone of control unless pinned. Entering an enemy zone ends movement; movement directly between enemy-controlled hexes is prohibited. You may withdraw to a free hex. Reserve orders last one turn: choose Reinforce or Counterattack, tap Assign reserve, then a friendly formation within three hexes. A ready reserve in command reacts up to two movement points to observed adjacent threats after normal movement. Counterattack may then attack an adjacent enemy. Tanks held in reserve consume two supplies. Pinned units or units with cohesion below 40 cannot react.

Cohesion: Operational combat disruption reduces cohesion as well as suppression. Low cohesion reduces firepower; below 40 it also reduces movement, prevents preparation, and prevents reserve reaction. Unhit units recover five cohesion while acting or fifteen while resting.

Combat estimates: Select Attack to see estimated firepower ratios, losses, return fire, cover, readiness, preparation, and attachments for currently spotted targets. Forecasts use current positions and planned supply; movement, bombardment, smoke, reactions, and enemy actions may change the result. Ratios are comparative firepower, not probabilities of victory.

Utah operations: With Utah events enabled, two illustrative airborne detachments appear inland after the first resolution. Infantry and engineers are due after one further turn; tanks and a field battery after three. No more than one formation lands in each beach sector per turn, and a sector with two occupied beach hexes delays landing. Clear beach sectors to admit follow-on waves. German infantry and armor arrive after an uncertain three-to-five-turn interval, subject to entry space. These replace the normal Utah follow-up infantry schedule; other scenarios retain their own reinforcement schedules.

Map size and holding box: Full-screen map fills the browser viewport, keeping Move, Attack, Rest, Resolve turn, air support, and Menu available. Tap Normal size to restore the dashboard; Escape also returns when no dialog is open. The compact action dock sits beside the map on a wide screen or underneath it in portrait. Unit details & advanced orders opens mission controls and the roster. The rear is a holding box below the map, not a second hex area. Open it to select a staged HQ. Choose Move, then a battlefield hex to advance it onto the map during resolution. Select a deployed HQ and Return HQ to holding box to withdraw it toward the rear using its normal movement allowance.

HQ deployment: HQs start east of the infantry/armor line: Regiment, then Division, Corps, and Army farther to the rear. The Rear holding box is abstract staging, separate from the 9 × 9 battlefield. Only HQs use it. HQs can move onto the battlefield through the eastern edge. Existing battles receive this setup correction once; subsequent HQ moves are retained.

Headquarters: Open Headquarters in the top header. The Regiment (III), Division (XX), Corps (XXX), and Army (XXXX) command posts are selectable counters; use Move to reposition them. The command desk shows ranges, headquarters condition, and each field formation’s reporting HQ. Select Show on map to inspect a command post and its range; Show command links displays the network. Assign formations directly to Regiment, Division, Corps, or Army from the desk. Command restrictions on reserve support apply with Advanced Rules. Headquarters cannot attack, return fire, capture flags, or prevent defeat after all field formations are eliminated.

Exit without saving: Use Exit game in the top header, then Exit without saving. This restores every scenario visited during this browser session to its starting save, including undoing this session’s autosaves, and displays an exit screen. Close the tab or choose Resume saved game. Safari may keep the tab open; the game does not force-close it.

Advanced Rules: Turn on the header toggle for suppression, preparation, and artillery missions. It is optional and saved separately in each scenario. Turning it off clears suppression, preparation, smoke, and advanced-only orders. Ammunition is retained.

Suppression: Levels 0–3 reduce firepower by 20% per level. Levels 2–3 also reduce movement by one. A formation hit this turn retains its suppression; otherwise it recovers one level while acting, or two while resting. Ground hits add one level. Suppression barrages add two even if they cause no strength losses. These rules apply to both sides.

Hasty or deliberate: Infantry and armor can attack immediately using Hasty, or choose Deliberate and target an adjacent enemy to spend a turn preparing. Next turn, keep Deliberate selected and target the same enemy again to attack with +2 losses. Moving, attacking, switching off Advanced Rules, or letting that next turn pass uses or cancels the preparation. A displaced, destroyed, or unspotted target may invalidate the attack.

Artillery missions: Batteries start with six ammunition points. Destructive fire uses two and adds one suppression. Suppression fire uses one, causes light losses, and adds two suppression. Both sides' barrages resolve together after movement and air strikes, before ground assaults. Destroyed ground units cannot attack. German batteries select destructive or suppression fire automatically. A resting battery with an open supply route and at least two supply reserves replenishes one ammunition point, up to six. Ammunition and supply reserves are separate; all firing batteries need at least one supply reserve.

Smoke and defensive fire: Select Smoke, tap Attack, then any land hex within four hexes. Smoke reduces incoming losses in that hex by two during this resolution, including your own fire against an enemy in smoke; it does not block spotting. Smoke clears at the next resolution. Select Defensive support, tap Attack, then a friendly counter within four hexes. The battery fires at one spotted enemy attacking that formation, provided the attacker is also within four hexes. If no eligible attacker appears, ammunition is retained. Mission readiness still consumes two supply points. Explicit artillery missions override inherited attachment orders.

Attachments: Open Attachments in the Orders panel. Assign on-map tanks or artillery to infantry; they retain their own counters, movement, supply, and range. If they have no order, they inherit their host’s attack at resolution; explicit orders take precedence. Outside Command mode, movement is ordered separately. In Command mode an attached field battery follows its host’s mission automatically; direct battery orders take precedence. Division, Corps, and Army each offer three finite off-map support units. Choose a receiving formation and use Attach, Reassign, or Release. Two field and two off-map attachments per host. Off-map bonuses, requirements, and current assignments appear in the support desk. Existing saves receive the new reserve pools automatically.

Combat: All movement occurs before fire. Bombardment resolves before ground assaults in both rulesets. Ground exchanges within their phase are simultaneous. Adjacent targets return fire against infantry or tanks. Cover and low strength reduce losses. Artillery causes no return fire. Use the Fog of war checkbox above the map to show or hide unspotted enemy units.

Supply Command: The header button opens a dedicated logistics desk. Choose My delivery requests to set 0–3 delivery points per unit. Requests repeat; High priority is served first, then Normal, then Low. Ties favor lower reserves then unit ID. Sustain this turn asks only for the points needed to pay current consumption; Rebuild reserves asks to fill each unit to 3. Both presets turn on manual requests. All entries share one scenario pool; unused points expire. Choose any, northern, central, or southern entry sector and shortest, road-favored, or cautious routing for each unit. Cautious routing avoids hexes next to observed enemies where practical; exposure is a warning, with no truck losses or road-capacity limits modeled. Forecasts assume current orders and positions. Fog may hide blockers that prevent an actual delivery.

Supply priorities: The Allied delivery allowance depends on the scenario; the supply panel shows the current limit. Each unit holds 0–3 reserve points, starting at 2. Supplies go to High, Normal, then Low priority, filling each unit to 3 before moving to the next. Ties favor lower reserves, then unit ID. A unit needs an unblocked land route to the eastern supply edge to receive supplies; enemy-occupied hexes block the route. The selected unit’s route is shown in blue dots. Your supply panel previews deliveries before you resolve. German supplies are managed automatically from their west edge.

Consumption: Every unit uses 1 point each turn, including resting units. Moving tanks and attacking artillery use 2. Deliveries arrive before movement and combat; consumption happens afterward. A connected unit may still be short of supplies if other units have higher priority. Unused deliveries expire each turn.

Readiness: With 2–3 reserve points after delivery, units have full firepower and movement. At 1, firepower falls to 80% and movement loses 1 point. At 0, firepower is 55% and movement loses 1 point. Movement always allows at least 1 point. Isolated units can use reserves until they run out; priority cannot restore a blocked route.

Fatigue & rest: Three fatigue points reduce movement by 1. Rest removes two fatigue and restores one strength only with an open route and at least 2 reserves after delivery. Rest still consumes supply.

Air support: Your scenario briefing sets the fighter-bomber mission allowance, with one strike per turn. Tap Assign air strike, then a German counter. You may change or cancel the target before resolving. Aircraft follow the targeted formation after movement and inflict 2–4 strength losses plus one fatigue before ground fire. A formation destroyed by air attack cannot fire. This first version has guaranteed air availability, with no weather or anti-aircraft simulation.

Reinforcements: Two Allied infantry detachments arrive at the turn specified by the scenario if the entry hexes are free. The situation report lists their expected arrival.

Mission command: Open Command missions from the header or full-screen Menu. Assign Seize or Defend an objective, Support a formation, or Reserve for a formation. Choose cautious, balanced, or aggressive risk. Orders persist across turns and reinforcements inherit nearby standing intent. Cyan dashed lines show intended movement; gold hexes remain the direct-control movement range. Issue a mission to one battalion or all maneuver formations. Direct moves and attacks override a mission for one turn; Rest / cancel pauses it. Cancel mission returns the formation to direct control. Turning Command mode off suspends all autonomous execution.

Authority: Attached reserve artillery and engineers provide their normal benefits automatically. Attached field batteries follow the host and fire without asking again. With Operational depth, mission engineers can repair an adjacent damaged crossing on the route. Range, supply, readiness, and applicable command links still matter. Default local delegation also authorizes free batteries sharing the requester’s HQ. Change to Ask about all additional support to approve those individually. Air strikes always require approval and an Army link. Only one air strike and one task per battery may be committed in a turn. Direct orders on a battery reserve it for your task.

Command review: Resolve interrupts only for support needing your approval. Approve the request, continue without it under the unit’s risk policy, or hold the formation. Continue commits the turn; Cancel leaves saves, positions, and resources untouched. You can always open a full next-turn review from the mission desk. Disconnected formations continue standing intent locally; they report why coordinated support is unavailable. New threats, hidden blockers, or enemy movement can invalidate a forecast.

Learning and reports: First game opens a six-step guide. Subordinate reports explain progress, blocked routes, recovery, and lost support targets. Combat dispatches show strength, readiness, cover, attachments, and (with Advanced Rules) preparation, suppression, and smoke. The scenario terrain and force mixes are schematic, illustrative designs.

Saving: Every order and turn saves in this browser when storage is available. Export a save for a backup or to transfer between devices. Safari’s Share → Add to Home Screen gives you an app-style shortcut. Sign in to ChatGPT if the private link asks.

Version 0.22: coordinated attacks and defense

In Mission Command, select the main effort, choose an objective, select supporting formations under Coordinated attack, and assign. Enable Command mode and review intentions. All elements must be ready and able to engage the same observed target; the main effort waits and reports delays. Direct orders override coordination. Resolve turn executes actual formation fire without a bonus multiplier.

Defend holds the current hex with one extra cover point and remains active until replaced. Rest / cancel ends it. Defenders consume normal supply and recover fatigue more slowly than resting units; they do not regain strength through rest while defending.

Select a friendly unit and use Command Visit for its current reported situation, readiness, orders and command link. The visit is a briefing and does not move headquarters or expose concealed enemies. Turn SITREPs and the activity log show actual progress and needs.

Estimated boundaries and frontage

Boundaries and frontage (v0.23.1): Use Boundaries above the map. Select infantry or armor to show gold left/right limits and flank reports under Unit details. Other maneuver units show blue fronts. A moving formation uses a narrow march column; attack and defense spread out. Green dotted links estimate contact; orange dashed links show gaps or staggered formations. L/R are relative to the direction of advance, inland by default. Estimates update with orders, missions, movement, strength, fatigue, suppression, supply and local terrain. Attached support uses its host frontage; HQs and artillery do not hold separate maneuver sectors. These are schematic estimates in map hex widths, not historical boundaries, guaranteed coverage, movement restrictions, or combat modifiers.
